Korean Marine Collagen PDP:
A CRO Breakdown

This document examines the Wellbeing Nutrition Korean Marine Collagen product page through a conversion lens — identifying where the existing page loses buyer confidence, proposing targeted experiments, and explaining the rationale behind the redesigned shopping experience.

Brand: Wellbeing Nutrition
Product: Korean Marine Collagen (200g)
Price: ~₹1,529
Focus: PDP Conversion Optimization
ℹ️ This analysis is based on a qualitative review of the live product page without access to real analytics, heatmaps, session recordings, or customer interviews. Funnel estimates and benchmark ranges are illustrative. All recommendations are hypotheses to be validated through testing.
Three Highest-Impact Changes
1
Rewrite the headline to lead with outcomes. The current title reads like a search string. Buyers need to immediately understand what the product does for them.
2
Surface the results timeline above the fold. "When will I see results?" is the top buyer question, but the answer is buried in the FAQ section.
3
Add trust signals directly below the price. Trust badges at the purchase decision point reduce hesitation where it matters most.
Section 01

🎯Problem Framing & Buyer Context

A ₹1,529 wellness supplement is not an impulse buy. The potential customer has already done some research, is aware of collagen as a category, and is evaluating Wellbeing Nutrition against alternatives (OZiva, HK Vitals, Kapiva). The product page is likely the primary decision environment — and it needs to do the job that a knowledgeable sales assistant would do in a store.

Section 02

🔍Existing PDP Audit

I reviewed the live product page at wellbeingnutrition.com like a potential buyer would — reading copy, checking information hierarchy, and noting what required additional effort to find. The audit below covers what is working, what is creating friction, and why each item matters for conversion.

What the Page Already Does Well

Strong Ingredient Story
Working

Deep-sea Korean fish sourcing, nano-hydrolyzed peptides (<1000 Daltons), and lab-testing claims are genuine differentiators. The information exists — it just needs earlier and more prominent placement in the page hierarchy.

Product Photography
Working

The product images on the live page are clean and well-lit. Multiple images show the packaging from different angles, which gives buyers sufficient visual confidence about what they are purchasing.

Clear Dosage Instructions
Working

How-to-use information is present. Daily dosage (5–10g), mixing instructions, and timing guidance are included — reducing post-purchase uncertainty for buyers who make it that far.

Comprehensive FAQ
Working

The FAQ section is detailed and covers the most common buyer objections — vegetarian status, weight gain, mixing with hot drinks, comparison with bovine collagen, and results timeline. The content is good; placement is the issue.

Friction Points and Information Hierarchy Issues

Keyword-Dump Headline
High Impact

The current title — "Pure Korean Marine Collagen Peptides | Unflavored | Combat Signs of Ageing" — reads like an SEO string, not a product benefit statement. It names chemical specs and format rather than communicating what changes for the buyer. Buyers are scanning for relevance in under 3 seconds.

Results Timeline Buried in FAQ
High Impact

"When will I see results?" is almost certainly the #1 buyer question in this category. The answer ("4–8 weeks") is accurate and stated in the FAQ — but requiring a buyer to scroll to the bottom and open an accordion to find it creates unnecessary uncertainty during the consideration phase.

No Trust Signals at Decision Point
High Impact

The price and Add-to-Cart button area has no surrounding reassurance messaging. Key information — lab tested, deep-sea sourced, money-back policy — appears either much higher on the page or not at all near the primary CTA. Conversion decisions are made at the point of maximum risk perception.

No Subscription or Repeat-Order Option
Revenue Leak

Collagen is a daily-use supplement with a 4–12 week efficacy window, meaning repeat purchases are the natural product behavior. The absence of a subscribe-and-save or bundle option near the purchase controls leaves potential LTV gains unaddressed.

Mobile CTA Scroll Distance
Mobile Issue

On mobile, reaching the Add-to-Cart button after reading even two sections of content requires significant scroll. A sticky or floating CTA bar on mobile would reduce friction for buyers who are ready to purchase mid-scroll.

Unanchored Social Proof
Trust Gap

Reviews appear to exist, but the review system (Judge.me) loads asynchronously. During the critical first few seconds, there is no visible star rating or review summary in the hero section — the part of the page that buyers see first.

Section 03

📌Prioritized CRO Opportunities

Each opportunity below maps a specific user problem to a proposed intervention, with an assessment of effort and expected direction of impact. Specific percentage lift predictions are excluded — without baseline data, any numbers would be fabricated.

Effort vs. Impact Overview

Opportunity H1
Quick Win Priority: High
Rewrite the product headline to lead with buyer outcome

Buyers scanning the page don't see an immediate answer to "what does this do for me?" The current title names the product format, not the benefit.

Replace title with an outcome-first headline (e.g., "Firmer Skin & Stronger Hair. In 8 Weeks."), with the technical product name as a sub-label.

Processing fluency principle: buyers make faster, more confident decisions when they can immediately map a product to a desired outcome.

Add-to-Cart rate (ATC%) — secondary: scroll depth past hero section.

Opportunity H2
Quick Win Priority: High
Place trust signals directly below the price and CTA

At the moment of maximum decision anxiety (price + buy button), there is no reassurance. Key differentiators are scattered elsewhere.

Add 4–5 compact trust indicators directly below the ATC button: lab-tested, deep-sea sourced, Dalton size, money-back policy, return terms.

Risk-reduction at the point of decision. The closer reassurance messaging is to the buy action, the more effective it is at reducing abandonment.

ATC-to-checkout rate (measures drop-off between cart addition and checkout initiation).

Opportunity H3
Medium Effort Priority: High
Add a visual "What to Expect" results timeline above the fold

The #1 anxiety with collagen is uncertainty about when it works. This information is buried in FAQ and requires scrolling and clicking to access.

A 4-step visual progression (Week 2 → Week 4 → Week 8 → Week 12) showing expected changes at each stage, placed in the hero or just below it.

Reduces ambiguity and sets honest expectations — both of which increase purchase commitment and reduce return rates. Anchors the buyer's mental model before they open competing tabs.

Time-on-page, CTA engagement rate, and return rate as a guardrail.

Opportunity H4
Quick Win Priority: Medium
Implement a sticky purchase bar on mobile

On mobile, buyers who are ready to purchase mid-scroll have to scroll back up or all the way down to find the Add-to-Cart button.

A compact bottom bar on mobile (visible after user scrolls past the hero) showing product name, price, and an ATC button.

Reduces conversion friction at peak intent moments — buyer doesn't need to navigate to act on their decision.

Mobile ATC rate specifically. Guardrail: layout shift / usability feedback.

Opportunity H5
Structural Priority: Medium (Long-term)
Introduce a Subscribe & Save purchase option

The product is a daily-use supplement with a minimum 8-week commitment for visible results. No repeat-purchase option is offered, which misses LTV potential and makes a two-bottle purchase feel like a large upfront commitment.

Add a two-option radio selector above ATC: "One-time purchase (₹1,529)" and "Subscribe monthly + save" — pending product team validation of feasibility and discount structure.

Subscription anchoring reduces perceived per-unit cost, aligns with natural product usage cycle, and improves LTV without needing to acquire new customers.

Subscription adoption rate, 90-day revenue per customer. Requires backend integration and product team input before testing.

Section 04

🎨Redesigned PDP — Strategy & Rationale

The redesigned product page (see enhanced-pdp.html) preserves the core content and brand identity while restructuring the information hierarchy to address the friction points identified above. Each major design decision is mapped to a specific user problem.

1. Outcome-first headline

The redesigned page opens with "Firmer Skin & Stronger Hair. In 8 Weeks." as the primary heading. Technical specs (Type I & III collagen, nano-hydrolyzed) appear in the first line below. This addresses H1 directly.

2. Trust pills in the hero

Compact trust indicators (deep-sea sourced, lab-tested, unflavored, keto-friendly) are placed directly below the product name in a pill-row format. They are visible before the buyer scrolls, answering basic source and safety questions upfront.

3. Results timeline as a dedicated section

A 4-step visual timeline (Weeks 2 → 4 → 8 → 12) surfaces the expected progression clearly and early in the page. This answers the #1 buyer question before they bounce to research elsewhere. Language is careful: "many users notice" rather than guaranteed outcomes.

4. Purchase-option selector

A two-option radio UI presents one-time purchase vs. a suggested subscription option. This is labeled as a design proposal, with prices marked as illustrative pending business model validation.

5. Sticky mobile ATC bar

A fixed bottom bar appears on mobile once the user scrolls past the product hero. It shows the product name, price, and an Add-to-Cart button — keeping the purchase action accessible regardless of scroll position.

6. Science section with qualified claims

The bioavailability claim is framed as "higher bioavailability versus standard collagen" rather than asserting a specific multiplier. The science section focuses on the mechanism (smaller peptide size, better gut absorption) rather than unverified clinical outcomes.

⚠️ The redesigned page uses actual product images from the Wellbeing Nutrition CDN for the gallery. Review content and rating data in the redesigned page are explicitly labeled as illustrative. Prices and subscription details are design proposals, not confirmed business decisions.

Section 08

⚠️Limitations & Next Steps

This analysis is based on a qualitative review of the product page and general D2C supplement category knowledge. A number of things would materially improve the quality and confidence of these recommendations.

📈
No Access to Real Analytics
All funnel estimates are illustrative benchmarks. The actual ATC rate, scroll depth, device split, and conversion rate for this specific PDP are unknown. Reviewing real GA4 data would immediately surface the biggest drop-off points.
🗺️
No Heatmap or Session Data
Without Clarity or Hotjar recordings, it's impossible to know precisely where buyers scroll, click, or stop. This would either validate or significantly revise the audit findings above.
🗣️
No Customer Research
The buyer segments and purchase objections above are assumptions. Even 5 user interviews (or a survey sent to past buyers) would reveal actual objections and make hypothesis prioritization much sharper.
🔗
Subscription Model Unverified
H5 (subscribe & save) assumes the business model and operations can support it. This would need product team, finance, and ops input before it can be designed or tested properly.
📱
Mobile Traffic Share Unknown
If the majority of traffic is mobile (common for D2C wellness brands in India), mobile-specific improvements (sticky CTA, image sizing, font scaling) would warrant higher priority than they currently have.
🧪
Traffic Volume Constrains Testing
Statistical significance requires sufficient traffic. If the PDP receives fewer than ~10,000 sessions per month, some experiments will take months to reach confidence. Sample size should be estimated before designing tests.

Recommended Immediate Next Steps

  • Confirm tracking setup: Verify GA4 is capturing ATC, scroll depth, FAQ opens, and checkout steps correctly. This is prerequisite for everything else.
  • Establish a 2-week baseline: Run no experiments until you have clean baseline data to compare against.
  • Run 3–5 buyer interviews: Even short calls with recent customers (or survey responses) would validate whether the identified friction points are real.
  • Prioritize mobile audit: Check the current PDP on Android and iOS at actual device dimensions. Identify any layout or usability issues that don't appear on desktop.
  • Start with headline test: Experiment 01 requires no backend work, can be run via a testing tool, and directly tests the highest-confidence hypothesis.
